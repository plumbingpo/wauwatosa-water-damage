Why Hire a Professional for Mold Remediation?

This is not a job to take on yourself. Without containment, scrubbing or tearing out moldy material releases spores by the millions, potentially spreading contamination through the house and making the health exposure worse than it was. Capturing and containing the spores during removal takes HEPA-filtered scrubbers, protective equipment for the crew and containment kept under negative air pressure, which is how professionals do it.

Just as important, we deal with moisture, the underlying cause of every mold problem. Clean off the visible growth but leave the moisture problem, and the mold comes back, in many cases in a matter of weeks. Finding concealed moisture, drying it properly and preventing a repeat all take equipment and training, which certified remediation technicians have.

Maintenance Tips for Mold Remediation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Aim for 30 to 50% indoor humidity and never let it stay above 60%, running dehumidifiers and ventilating as needed
  • Use mold-resistant drywall and paint in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and basements
  • A vapor barrier is worth considering in a crawl space, which also needs correct ventilation
  • Hang or launder wet fabrics promptly and wipe up spills as they happen
  • Repair any leak inside 24 hours, since 24 to 48 hours of moisture is all mold requires to begin
  • Check that exhaust fans in the kitchen, laundry room and bathrooms move enough air

Warning Signs for Mold Remediation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Moisture beading up again and again on glass, walls or anything cold
  • Signs of concealed moisture such as paint that bubbles, wallpaper that peels or materials that warp
  • Feeling better away from home, then having coughs, headaches or allergies come back indoors
  • Patches of mold, whatever the color, on a ceiling, wall, floor or other surface
  • Stains spreading on a ceiling or wall, or dark marks ringing the air vents
  • Spots you wipe away that keep reappearing in the same place
  • An earthy, musty smell that will not go away, mostly in humid or closed-off spaces
