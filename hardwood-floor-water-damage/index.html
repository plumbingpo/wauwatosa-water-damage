Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Wauwatosa Homeowners Should Know

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. That does not mean the floor is lost. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. Success depends on a slow, even drying rate that draws water from the flooring and from the subfloor beneath it.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

Warning Signs

  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
  • Blackish or gray discoloration at board joints and fasteners
  • Cloudy white patches or peeling topcoat over the damp section
  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
  • Planks pulling loose from the subfloor or peaking where they meet
  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
Our Process

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Remove Surface Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Monitor and Advise

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Our final advice is one of three outcomes, no further work, swapping a few boards, or refinishing the floor.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

After a week

Beyond Drying

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. At this stage most of the wet area needs to be replaced rather than dried.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Cracks From Rushed Drying

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Moisture Under the Boards

Long after the boards seem dry, the subfloor may still be holding water. If it is not dried too, the floor will cup again or grow mold from underneath.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

Growth Beneath the Floor

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.
