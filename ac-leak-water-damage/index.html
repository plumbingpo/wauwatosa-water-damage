AC Leak Water Damage Help in Wauwatosa

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Insulation Around the Unit

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Leaks decay the stand and climb the closet drywall, concealed by the equipment itself.

Ceilings Under the Unit

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Swollen fiberboard pieces do not recover and are normally swapped for new ones.

What We Do

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Extract and Open Up

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Days-old condensate is not clean water, so everything it reached is treated with an antimicrobial. Then we replace insulation, drywall, baseboard and flooring and repaint so the area matches.

Coverage

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.
