Wet Carpet Drying: What Wauwatosa Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Wauwatosa extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • The damp area gets wider over time with no new spill to explain it
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Swollen Subfloor

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.
