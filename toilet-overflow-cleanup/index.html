Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in Wauwatosa

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. Some slips beneath the baseboard and into the wall, and some drops through the opening around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Water that came up through the bowl is not, and it has to be handled as contaminated.

The first thing our Wauwatosa team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off the SupplyTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Stop FlushingNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Check the Room BelowFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Coverage

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
  • Waste rising out of the lowest toilet in the house because the main sewer line is blocked.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • Clean water flooding the bathroom from a supply line that failed or a tank that cracked.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Baseboards and door casings sit right at floor level and soak up water from the bottom edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a second story bathroom, water travels down along the drain pipe and collects on top of the ceiling drywall below, which stains, sags and in some cases collapses.

Wet Vanity and Wall Bases

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. Several inches of wall above floor level can hold moisture with no visible sign on the paint.

Bacteria in Building Materials

Water from the drain or bowl deposits bacteria in drywall, baseboards, carpet and pad. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Determine the Water Type

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

We clean the subfloor, fixtures, wall bases and floors, then treat them with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Drying and Final Readings

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. Our crew keeps metering the subfloor and the ceiling until both read normal again.
