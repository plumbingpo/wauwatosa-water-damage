What Usually Goes Wrong in Madison Homes

Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. The five below are ones we see constantly in Madison, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. This water has crossed lawns, driveways and streets, which makes it unsanitary. The Wauwatosa Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. In Madison kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. How Drying Works on a Madison Water Damage Job

A wet building left alone does dry eventually, but far too slowly. Left alone, water trapped in drywall, wood framing and subfloor can stay for weeks, which is long enough for mold and warping. Our job on every Madison property is to speed that up in a controlled way.

First the liquid water comes out, because extraction removes far more water per hour than evaporation ever will. Next, air movers placed around the room drive air over damp materials so the water in them evaporates. Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and send it down a drain, which keeps the room dry enough for evaporation to continue. Air movers on their own only stir humid air, so the two machines work as a pair.

A technician measures the wet materials daily against readings from an unaffected area of the property. For most Madison homes the target is met in 3 to 5 days, though hardwood, plaster and other dense materials need more time. We remove the machines when the readings reach the goal, and not before.

How Madison Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A flood in the house feels like it came from nowhere, yet most trace back to wear that built up over months or years. For homeowners in Madison, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Plumbing comes first. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Next come appliances.
