Kitchen Water Damage Help in Wauwatosa

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. For homeowners in Wauwatosa, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.
