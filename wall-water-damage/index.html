Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Wauwatosa Homeowners Should Know

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Signs to Look For

  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Growth Behind the Paint

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Insulation Holding Water

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. Drying from inside the room is not enough when the insulation is soaked.

Repair or Replace

What Stays and What Goes

We save what can be dried properly and remove only what cannot. Moisture readings make that call, not guesswork.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
