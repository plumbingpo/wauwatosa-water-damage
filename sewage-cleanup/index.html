Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Wauwatosa, Wauwatosa Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
