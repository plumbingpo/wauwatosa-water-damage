Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Wauwatosa, WI

Home / Blog / Preventing Sewage Backups in Wauwatosa, WI

Few household emergencies are as unpleasant as sewage backing up into the home. Raw sewage entering your living spaces creates immediate health hazards, causes extensive property damage, and requires professional cleanup that can cost thousands of dollars. Understanding why sewage backups occur and taking preventive measures can significantly reduce your risk. Below, Wauwatosa Restoration explains what usually causes them for Wauwatosa, WI homeowners, plus the practical things you can do to protect your home.

What Causes a Sewer to Back Up

A backup happens when wastewater leaving your property cannot reach the septic tank or the municipal sewer system the way it should. Most cases trace back to the following: sewer pipes that have aged or collapsed, roots from trees intruding into the pipe, grease, hair, and debris clogging the line, heavy rainfall overloading the municipal sewers, a combined sewer overflow event, or improper items going down the toilet, including excessive paper, wipes, and feminine products.

Tree Root Intrusion

Tree roots are one of the most common causes of sewer line blockages in Wauwatosa. A sewer pipe is a steady supply of moisture, which is exactly what roots go looking for. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. The risk is highest with aging clay tile pipe, which roots penetrate readily. Having your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years can identify root problems before they cause a backup.

Ways to Lower Your Backup Risk

To guard a Wauwatosa house against backups, start by having a backwater valve fitted to your sewer connection so the city system cannot flow back in. Grease and cooking oil should never go down a drain. Toilet paper is the only thing that belongs in the toilet, so no wipes, no feminine products, and nothing else. Schedule regular sewer line inspections and cleaning. Deal with a slow drain quickly, before it turns into a full blockage. Know the location of your sewer cleanout for emergency access. It may also be worth looking at sewer line coverage or a backup endorsement for your homeowner's insurance.

What to Do If Sewage Backs Up

When sewage backs up, leave the cleanup alone. It carries viruses, bacteria, parasites, and other dangerous pathogens capable of making you seriously ill.
