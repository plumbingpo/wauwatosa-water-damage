Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Wauwatosa

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Wauwatosa crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Leave the Door ShutDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Refrigerators that leak at floor level because the drain pan has cracked or the defrost drain is clogged.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Kitchen Cabinets

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Certain insurers will want to know the hose's age and whether it was kept up. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

  • Whether cabinets and flooring can be dried in place or need to be removed.

  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
