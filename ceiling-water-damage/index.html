Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Wauwatosa Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

What It Looks Like

  • Stains or streaks running down the wall just below the ceiling line
  • Seams, tape lines and fastener heads becoming visible across the ceiling
  • Ceiling paint lifting off in blisters or peeling strips
  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Dark spots or speckled growth forming on the ceiling surface
  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

Wet Lights and Wiring

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

Sudden Collapse

Wet drywall is far heavier than dry and loses its grip on the fasteners. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

What We Do

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Secure the Room

We cut power to any wet fixture and move or cover the furniture underneath. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Open and Dry

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

The insulation gets wet as water travels down the framing. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

Drywall softens and pulls away from its screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.
