Common Water Damage Issues in Green Bay

Walk through a house from the roof down and you pass most of the places a leak can begin. Our crews in Green Bay follow that same path on an inspection, and these five stops cause the most trouble.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Insulation acts like a sponge, so it can hold a surprising amount before any of it shows on the ceiling. Wet insulation stops insulating and keeps the wood beneath it damp. We take out the ruined material, dry the framing and leave the roof repair itself to a roofer.

Bathrooms: A tub left filling, a sink with a blocked overflow hole or a toilet that runs over can soak a bathroom floor quickly. Tile looks waterproof, yet water gets through grout lines and around the edges of the room. When the bathroom is on an upper story, the water shows up as a wet patch overhead downstairs. If the toilet bowl was the source, assume the water is unsanitary and have the area disinfected.

Walls: Supply lines and drain pipes run inside the walls, and a slow leak there can go on for a long time. You might see trim pulling away, a discolored spot spreading or drywall that gives when pressed. Our Green Bay technicians scan the area with infrared and probe it with a meter to mark the wet zone. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: A water heater holds a large volume under pressure, usually in a closet, garage or utility room. Worn parts announce themselves with moisture at the top connections, the drain spout or the pressure relief pipe. When the tank itself fails, close the cold water valve above it and switch off the gas or power to the unit. Our Wauwatosa Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. Dark water rising in a floor drain or a ground-level shower means the main line is not carrying waste away. That water is Category 3, the most contaminated kind, and it takes protective equipment to clean up safely. Hold off on flushing or running taps, and keep the household clear while our team heads to Green Bay.

What to Do Before Our Green Bay Crew Arrives

The first hour is when good intentions cause the most trouble. These are the mistakes our crews see most often in Green Bay, and what to do instead.

Never run an ordinary vacuum cleaner over a wet floor. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Leave fitted carpet in place, because pulling it up the wrong way can stretch or rip a carpet we might have saved. Do not turn the heat up high to dry things out, because warm, damp air is what mold likes.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Do take pictures before moving anything. Keep kids and animals out until it has been checked. And do make the call early, so Wauwatosa Restoration can have a crew on the way to Green Bay while you do the rest.

How Green Bay Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Green Bay homes. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Renew the caulk around baths and showers once it splits, because water that slips behind tile stays hidden for a long time. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Green Bay home full of drying machines for most of a week.
